I — Preface —
A NOTE FROM THE STUDIO

A decade of refusing the brief as written.

This volume collects ten years of work that, strictly speaking, should not have been made. The clients came with briefs. The briefs asked for clarity, friendliness, approachability — words that mean nothing and prohibit everything. We answered with systems that refuse to settle, with marks that change between meetings, with type that is, in places, deliberately difficult to read. The work collected here is what survived that refusal.

There is no house style. There are house habits: a preference for the slow reveal over the loud announcement, for the serif over the sans, for the debossed edge over the spot-varnish. We have, in eleven years, used the colour gold perhaps too readily. We have not, in eleven years, used a single gradient. These are not principles. They are observations, made in hindsight, of the work that finally left the studio.

The pages that follow are arranged not by chronology, nor by client prestige, but by the order in which the studio learned to stop apologising for the work. The first project collected here was the moment we understood that a logo could be a language and not a signature. The last was the moment we understood that a book can disagree with its reader and still be loved. Everything between is the long argument between those two positions.

What follows is offered without apology and without instruction. The work is not for everyone. It was never meant to be.

Severin Halbe
Studio Halbe · Leipzig · March 2024

01
Kunsthaus Nord Identity & Wayfinding System · 2023

A museum that refuses to settle on a single face.

A regional museum of contemporary craft asked for "a logo." We delivered a generative system of 64 marks — one for each wing, donor tier, and rotating exhibition — that the museum continues, four years later, to expand.

Client Kunsthaus Nord, Rostock
Year 2023
Scope Identity, signage, type
Award TDC NY · Bronze

The brief was modest: a mark for a regional museum of contemporary craft. The answer was less so. We built the identity from a single eight-by-eight grid that yields sixty-four possible glyphs — one for each permanent collection wing, each rotating exhibition, each donor tier, each educational program.

The mark never appears the same way twice. Visitors learn to read it as language, not logo. Members write us letters complaining when a particular favourite glyph is rotated out of circulation. That, we feel, is the correct relationship between an institution and its public.

A logo is a sentence the institution keeps finishing.

The wordmark, by contrast, is fixed: a single weight of Stempel Garamond redrawn at 7 degrees of optical correction, set always at the same size, always in the same gold. The system's stillness sits inside its own noise.

02
Hex & Oak Distillery Packaging & Art Direction · 2022

How to make a three-hundred-and-forty dollar bottle feel like an heirloom.

Hex & Oak came to us with one barrel of single-malt and the question of how to make a luxury bottle feel inherited rather than purchased. We answered with paper, wax, and the deliberate refusal of every available finishing technique.

Client Hex & Oak, Highland
Year 2022
Edition 312 bottles
Stock 240gsm Cotton, Hahnemühle
PL. 02.A — BOTTLE NO. 137 / 312 FULL COLOUR · DEBOSSED

Hex & Oak came to us with one barrel of single-malt and the question of how to make a $340 bottle feel like an heirloom rather than a luxury. We answered with paper — not embossing, not foil, not the spot-UV that every other distillery of that price point had reached for.

A 240gsm cotton stock from a Bavarian mill founded in 1883. Debossed with a steel die cut from the same drawing that produced the wordmark. Hand-waxed at the edge in a colour mixed to match the whisky itself, sampled from cask number one.

The label is the bottle's only ornament. The wax is the seal. Everything else is restraint.

The result, when held, has the slight give of an old book cover. The result, when read, has the slight difficulty of one. We were asked, twice, to make the wordmark "friendlier." We declined, twice. The whisky sold out in eleven weeks.

Bottle 700ml · Hand-blown
Wax Cask-toned · Beeswax
Type Hex & Oak Antiqua
Finish Deboss · No Foil

03
Frequenz 24 Poster Series · 2024

Twelve nights of music, twelve typographic arguments.

A poster series for an avant-garde electronic festival in which the tracking of each headline is set, mechanically, by the BPM of that night's headline act. Slow music reads wide. Fast music reads compressed. The fastest night is nearly unreadable. That is the point.

Client Frequenz Festival, Berlin
Year 2024
Output 12 posters · A1
Print Risograph · 2 colour

Twelve posters for twelve nights of avant-garde electronic music. We commissioned no photography. The brief became a typographic system: a single weight of Akzidenz Grotesk, set always at forty-eight point, modulated only by the beats-per-minute of each night's headline act.

SEVERIN HALBE / MONOGRAPH III CHAPTER 04 — RAUHBART P. 122—157
04
Rauhbart Typeface · 2023

A typeface rescued from a defunct East German sign-painter's manual.

Rauhbart began as a salvage job: digitising the chopped, awkward display letters of a 1962 Saxon sign-painter's reference. We spent eleven months deciding what to add. The answer, in the end, was almost nothing.

Released Spring 2023
Weights One (Display)
Glyphs 472, Latin Extended
Foundry Halbe Type
— PLATE 04.A — RAUHBART · 240PT
Ra
— one weight, one alternate, one opinion —

Rauhbart began as a rescue mission: the cut-by-hand display letters of a defunct East German sign-painter's manual, photographed at the Brno Moravian Archive in the spring of 2022. We digitised 242 characters from rubbings, then spent eleven months deciding what to add.

The answer was: almost nothing. Rauhbart ships in a single weight, with exactly one alternate (a long-s), and a deliberately small glyph set of 472 characters. It refuses most languages. It will not be expanded into a family. It does not have a "matching italic." The italic is Rauhbart tilted five degrees to the right, because that is what the original sign-painter did, and we are not smarter than him.

It is finished. The release notes contain the sentence: "We will not be revisiting this."

Das Buch der Geräte Editorial & Book Design · 2021

A four-hundred-and-eighty page argument with its own reader.

A hardback on the history of household tools, written by an academic who hates the word "designer." We set it in 9.4 point Stempel Garamond on a 14 point leading, with deliberately uneven margins, because the book wants to be held, and it wants, gently, to be disagreed with.

A 480-page hardback on the history of household tools, written by an academic who hates the word "designer." We were the third studio to take the project. The first two had been fired for proposing, respectively, a "modern minimalist cover" and a "playful typographic system." Both were correct proposals. Both missed the book.

We set it in 9.4pt Stempel Garamond on a 14pt leading, with a 92mm measure. The margins are deliberately uneven — wider at the foot than the head, wider at the thumb than the spine. The book wants to be held in the right hand. The book also wants, gently, to be disagreed with.

The margins are uneven because the hand that holds the book is uneven. A book that ignores the hand that holds it is not designed. It is merely typeset.

The cover carries no photograph, no illustration, no blurb. Only the title, set in a single weight of foundry type, debossed into a clay-toned cloth. The author's name appears, in 9 point, on the inside front flap. He preferred it that way. So did we.

VI — Lectures —
— VI —

Lectures & Talks.

Three on-the-record appearances from 2022–2024. The arguments have not, in the intervening months, been retracted.

BERLIN · 2024 47:22
BRAND NEW CONF.14 SEP 2024

On the discipline of refusal.

Forty-seven minutes on why the most useful sentence in a designer's vocabulary is "we would prefer not to." Includes three case studies of briefs that improved once the client was told no.

LEIPZIG · 2023 52:08
TYPO TALKS06 MAY 2023

Against the modular grid.

A deliberately provocative argument that the twelve-column grid has, over twenty years, made digital design measurably more boring. Proposes, with examples, six alternatives — none of them scalable.

BARCELONA · 2022 38:44
OFFF FESTIVAL13 APR 2022

The last good year for print.

A eulogy, delivered half in earnest, for the year 2011 — the last year, by the studio's count, in which a printed object could credibly claim to be the primary medium of an idea. Ends with a list of things we miss.
